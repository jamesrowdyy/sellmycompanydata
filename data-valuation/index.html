How the data valuation calculator works

Our free calculator gives an indicative licence range from four answers: full-time employees, year founded, where the company is based and the systems your team uses. It runs in your browser. It does not inspect your records and it is not a buyer offer.

Where the numbers come from

Most buyers publish price ranges, not formulas. micro1 publishes tiers starting at $100K for companies with 30 or more staff, and Polyshares quotes $100K to $2M+. Handshake AI goes further: its data-partnership calculator publishes the pricing behind its estimates. We use that pricing as our base, because it is the most detailed public buyer figure available.

The calculation

A company's value starts at $140,000, plus $2,333 per employee, plus $11,667 per year of operation, plus $15.75 per gigabyte of records. The low end of the range takes 75% of that total and the high end takes 125%. Both ends are then adjusted for where the company is based and rounded up by 1%. Below 50 staff, the low end is reduced by up to $69,102, the full amount at 20 staff and nothing from 50 staff up.

Record volume is where the systems come in. The low end always assumes 1 GB of records per employee per year. The high end rises with each system you select, from 1 GB with none selected to 14 GB at six or more, the top of the published range. More systems means more records, so the top of your range goes up.

Team size counts up to 200 people and history up to 20 years. Above either, the range shows a plus sign, because larger and older companies are priced individually. Published programmes go past $4M.

Who qualifies

Buyers license from companies with at least 20 employees and at least 3 years of operation. Below that, the calculator says so instead of showing a number. Some buyers want 30 or more staff, so a company with 20 to 29 people can expect fewer bidders.

What does not change the number

Revenue and industry. No buyer publishes pricing on either. Industry still matters to the conversation: client, patient and customer files stay out of any licence, and some sectors, like chemistry, chip design and manufacturing, are in demand right now. The calculator shows a note when that applies.

What happens before you answer?

Until you touch the calculator, it shows a labelled example: a 50-person US company, 10 years old, on three systems. Once you start, it asks for your team size and year founded, and updates as you go.
